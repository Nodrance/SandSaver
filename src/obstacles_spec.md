Obstacles:
- Stream: Streams are not obstacles, but are a definition used in this document while defining how obstacles behave. Any place where sand falls off an edge or is created at the top is a stream. If a lot of sand is falling off an edge (several streams or a very dense one), the stream will be wide. A wide stream is also one from the top that's wide, regardless of how dense it is. A wide stream is also a funnel with a wide opening
- Slope: A thin rectangle with any angle >30 and <60. More likely to generate under streams, redirecting them. Likes to redirect streams "backwards" compared to how they fell off. As it creates a stream at its edge, this can cause cascading streams that go back and forth down the screen. Sometimes generates at the edge of a wide stream, splitting it.
- Square/diamond: Always rotated onto a corner so both top sides are at slope angles. More likely to generate under wide streams, splitting them
- Circle: Also likes to split wide streams
- Funnel: Two slopes facing each other, with a gap in the middle. The edges are "cut" vertically making the slopes parallelograms rather than rectangles. Likes to generate where several streams are close together, gathering and mixing them. Usually generates with the two sides at a 45 degree angle, but can be anywhere between 30 and 60 degrees. The two halves are usually at roughly the same height, but can be offset further.
- Long Hole: A long thin rectangle at a random vertical rotation between -45 and 45, cutting into existing shapes and opening up gaps in them. Tries to cut entirely through a shape if it can, rather than leaving a rectangular crater, but it can still do that. Long Holes like to generate under streams, creating a stream that falls through the hole and onto other obstacles. 
- Circle Hole: A circle that cuts into a shape, making a sort of cup. Sometimes it will cut through a shape entirely, but more often it will just make a cup.


Streams and obstacles are generated in such a way that they're influenced by each other, for example streams generate above obstacles, obstacles generate under streams, and obstacles generate so that they create streams that fall onto other obstacles, but they avoid loops where a stream disappears and then immediately decides the best place to be recreated is where it just was. Obstacles try to avoid overlapping. 
There's plenty of random variance, sometimes obstacles and streams break their own rules or generate in ways that don't make sense, in order to make the simulation more interesting.
